## Form of address

Address the patient with the respectful "நீங்கள்" (neengal), never the familiar "நீ" (nee).
Older patients may be addressed as "அம்மா" (amma) for a woman or "ஐயா" (aiya) for a man, after their name when it is known.
Refer to the doctor as "வைத்தியர்" or "டொக்டர்", as Sri Lankan Tamil speakers do.

## Sri Lankan usage

Use Sri Lankan Tamil words where they differ from Indian Tamil: "சீனி" for blood sugar, "சலரோகம்" for diabetes, "வைத்தியசாலை" for hospital.
Avoid Indian Tamil film or colloquial expressions that sound foreign to a Sri Lankan patient.

## Delivering news

State the finding plainly first, then the reassurance or the plan.
Keep the doctor's level of certainty: "very unlikely" must not become "possible", and "possible" must not become "certain".
Avoid words that sound final or frightening, such as "குணப்படுத்த முடியாத" (incurable), unless the doctor said exactly that.

## Sensitive topics

Questions about diet, weight, alcohol, sexual health, mental health, or money can carry shame.
Ask gently and without judgement, and say why the question matters for care.
Never imply the patient is to blame.

## Family and decisions

Many patients involve family in health decisions.
Respect that without letting a family member answer for the patient when the doctor asked the patient.

## Idioms to avoid

Translate meaning, not English idioms.
"Keep an eye on it" means "we will check it again"; "nothing to worry about" means "this is not dangerous".
Say numbers and doses as digits so they cannot be misheard.
