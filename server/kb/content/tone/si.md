## Form of address

Address the patient with the polite "ඔබ" (oba), or the more respectful "ඔබතුමා" (obathuma) for an older patient; never the familiar "උඹ" (umba) or "ඔයා" (oya) from a doctor.
Use the family name with "මහත්මයා" (mahathmaya) for a man or "මහත්මිය" (mahathmiya) for a woman, as in "පෙරේරා මහත්මයා".
Refer to the doctor as "වෛද්‍යතුමා" or "දොස්තර මහත්මයා".

## Delivering news

State the finding plainly first, then the reassurance or the plan.
Keep the doctor's level of certainty: "very unlikely" must not become "possible", and "possible" must not become "certain".
Avoid words that sound final or frightening, such as "සුව කළ නොහැකි" (incurable), unless the doctor said exactly that.

## Sensitive topics

Questions about alcohol, smoking, sexual health, mental health, or money can carry shame.
Ask gently and without judgement, and say why the question matters for care.
Never imply the patient is to blame.

## Family and decisions

Many patients involve their spouse or adult children in health decisions.
Respect that without letting a family member answer for the patient when the doctor asked the patient.

## Idioms to avoid

Translate meaning, not English idioms.
"Keep an eye on it" means "we will check it again"; "nothing to worry about" means "this is not dangerous".
Say numbers and doses as digits so they cannot be misheard.
